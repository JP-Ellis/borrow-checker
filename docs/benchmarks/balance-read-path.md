subject to the hardware caveat below.

benchmark. Fixture generation times below are carried over from the prior

**T0→T1 lengthens history at constant transaction density (24→120 months,
same 200 tx/month); T1→T2 holds history constant and multiplies density 10×
(200→2000 tx/month).** These are two different axes of scale, and the results
table below must be read accordingly — see the caveats after the table.

Note for context, not a read-path result: ledger *generation* was mildly
superlinear at T2 (344.01 s actual vs. a 246.63 s linear extrapolation from
T1, ~40% over — recorded in Task 5). That is write-path/generator behaviour,
not evidence about the read path measured here.

## Interpretation caveats

**1. The tiers vary two different things, and the two per-account benchmarks
respond differently.**

- `posting_buckets` requests a fixed, bounded 6-month window on the dominant
  account. If its cost were purely a function of window size, T0≈T1 would be
  the expected result (same window width, only total history differs), and
  only T1→T2 (10× density inside the same window) would be informative about
  scaling.
- **That expectation does not hold in the measured data.** `posting_buckets`
  grew ~10.8× from T0 (21.700 ms) to T1 (234.55 ms) — a bigger jump than its
  ~9.8× growth from T1 (234.55 ms) to T2 (2.3022 s), even though T0→T1 only
  lengthens *history* and T1→T2 is the one that multiplies *density* inside
  the window. A benchmark reading only a bounded recent window should not
  scale with total ledger history at all. The fact that it does is consistent
  with #370's claim that the underlying residual query has no date predicate
  and instead loads an account's entire elided-posting history regardless of
  the caller's window — so growing the dominant account's total history (T0→T1)
  costs just as much as growing the density inside the requested window
  (T1→T2). This is read as corroborating evidence for #370, not as a separate
  finding.
- `account_period_stats` additionally performs an *unbounded*
  `genesis..from` opening-balance load by design, so it is expected to scale
  with total history regardless of window. It does: 52.623 ms → 324.36 ms →
  3.5870 s, roughly linear-to-slightly-superlinear across both axes (T0→T1
  ≈6.2×, T1→T2 ≈11.1×).

**2. The per-account benchmarks target the dominant account deliberately.**
The generator concentrates ~30% of postings on one account (its `--skew`
knob); `balance_for`, `account_period_stats`, and `posting_buckets` all
target that account specifically. These numbers represent a realistic worst
case for a single busy account, not a typical account on this ledger —
`default_balances` and `net_worth`, which sum every account, are the whole-
ledger figures.

**3. Hardware and variance.** All numbers come from a single run on one
machine, and criterion's own bracket (`[lower, upper]`) reflects real sample
variance — several T1/T2 samples show wide brackets (e.g.
`default_balances/t1`: 566–727 ms) or logged low/high outliers. Treat every
figure above as an estimate with the stated bracket, not a precise point
value, and expect different absolute numbers (though probably similar
ratios) on different hardware.

## Verdicts

Criteria fixed in advance; see the design spec §6.

- **#370 — date-scope the residual query.** Justified if
  `account_period_stats` at T1 exceeds ~50 ms, or scales worse than linearly.
  **Measured: `account_period_stats` at T1 = 324.36 ms, ~6.5× the 50 ms
  threshold → JUSTIFIED.**
- **#362 — set-based `net_worth` and `report`.** Justified if `net_worth` at T1
  exceeds ~100 ms, or exceeds `default_balances` by more than ~2×.
  **Measured: `net_worth` at T1 = 578.30 ms, ~5.8× the 100 ms threshold
  (it does not separately exceed `default_balances`'s 645.98 ms at T1 — the
  first branch alone already clears the bar) → JUSTIFIED.**
- **#244 — populate the balances cache.** Justified only if `default_balances`
  itself exceeds ~100 ms at T1. **Measured: `default_balances` at T1 =
  645.98 ms, ~6.5× the 100 ms threshold → JUSTIFIED.**

All three verdicts are unambiguous at T1 — each measured figure clears its
threshold by a margin of roughly 5–7×, well outside the criterion's own
variance brackets. No verdict here turned on interpretation or a close call.
